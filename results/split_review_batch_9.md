## Case 161

**Split:** eval

**Dates found in question:** July 1, 2024, June 15, 2024

**Parsed dates:** 2024-06-15, 2024-07-01

**Question:**
Does BNSS Section 209 apply to an offense committed in Pakistan on June 15, 2024, and what is the temporal significance of the July 1, 2024 cutoff?

**Assistant reply:**
BNSS Section 209 would not apply to an offense committed in Pakistan on June 15, 2024, because the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita 2023 came into force on July 1, 2024. For offenses committed before this date, the equivalent provision was Section 188 of the Code of Criminal Procedure 1973, which dealt with the manner of taking evidence in inquiries and trials for offenses committed outside India. The CrPC 1973 Section 188 allowed for commissions to examine witnesses abroad and reception of evidence taken under such commissions. The July 1, 2024 cutoff is significant as it marks the repeal of CrPC 1973 and the commencement of BNSS 2023; post-July 1, 2024 offenses committed outside India are governed by BNSS Sections 208-209 for jurisdiction and evidence reception, while pre-July 1, 2024 offenses remain governed by CrPC 1973 Sections 188 and related provisions.

Legal Analysis (IRAC):
Issue: Does BNSS 209 apply to a June 15, 2024 offense in Pakistan?
Rule: BNSS 2023 came into force July 1, 2024 per Section 1. Pre-July 1 offenses governed by CrPC 1973. CrPC Section 188 was the equivalent provision for foreign evidence.
Application: June 15, 2024 predates July 1, 2024, so CrPC 1973 Section 188 applies, not BNSS 209. The procedural mechanism would be commission-based examination under CrPC rather than Central Government direction under BNSS.
Conclusion: BNSS 209 does not apply retrospectively; CrPC 188 governed pre-July 1, 2024 extraterritorial evidence reception.

Core legal principle: BNSS 209 applies prospectively from July 1, 2024; pre-July 1, 2024 extraterritorial offenses are governed by CrPC 1973 Section 188 with its commission-based evidence reception mechanism.
